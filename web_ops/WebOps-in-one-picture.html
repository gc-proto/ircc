WebOps as a transit network

Every request rides a line. The line decides which stops it makes. And what we measure after launch finds the next request.

One front door

Every request comes in through the same form. One triager picks its line and its priority (P1 to P3), so work stops depending on who you know.

The line decides the stops

A text change rides Express straight to build. Custom code adds design. A rethink adds research too. Simple work stays light; complex work gets the attention it needs.

Measuring closes the loop

Launch isn't the end. A monthly outcome report shows which pages work for people, and anything that doesn't goes back to triage as a new trip.

Network services

The regular reports and meetings that keep the network running. The ones marked with a yellow dot feed the loop line: they find the next requests.

Every week

  • Top-task alertsRAD · daily, automatic. A top-task page leaves its normal range and its owner hears that week.
  • Triage meetingTriager and leads · Mondays. Reviews the queue, P1s and late requests.

Every month

  • Outcome reportRAD · automatic. Web stats, page feedback, searches, Google search and task success, with problem pages flagged.
  • Owner reviewsRAD and each WUX Assistant Director. Each flag gets a decision: fix, plan or accept.
  • Operations reportPublishing · automatic. What WebOps produced: requests, service levels, backlog.

Every quarter

  • Task success reportRAD. Completion and ease for top tasks.
  • Lifecycle sweepEnterprise and owners. Keep, improve, merge or retire old pages.
  • Library reviewRAD and Enterprise. Proven patterns join the library.
  • Capacity checkWebOps manager. Is the work balanced?

Every year

  • Top-task refreshRAD and WUX advisors. Which tasks matter most.
  • Inventory auditEnterprise. Every page, its owner and its purpose.
  • RetrospectiveEveryone. What to keep, change or drop.
